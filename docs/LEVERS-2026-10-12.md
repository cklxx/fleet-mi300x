# DeepSeek decode: where the microseconds are, and what can still move (2026-10-12)

Everything here is measured on this repository's own machine unless marked otherwise. The shipped build
re-measured **3.5416–3.5471 ms device / 3.9288–3.9745 ms wall per token** (7 runs, 32/32 tokens, 27/27 layers)
in `results/phase-table-2026-10-12.md`.

## 1. The account

| section | microseconds | share | basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| step, traced | 3576.8 | — | device clock, one trace |
| step, un traced baseline | 3542–3547 | — | 6 free-running runs, 5 us spread |
| prologue: embed + the one dense layer | 127.5 | 3.6% | trace |
| core loop: 27 MoE layers | 3206 | 89.6% | trace |
| tail: last attention → argmax | 243 | 6.8% | trace |
| bytes at the measured streamed rate | 1238 | 34.6% of 3542 | 5.20 GB/token at 4.2 TB/s |
| **not bytes** | **~2300** | **65%** | difference of the two rows above |

Two facts decide the strategy:

1. **The step is not bandwidth-bound.** Bytes need 1.24 ms; the step takes 3.54 ms. Even at a perfect byte
   pipeline the step would be set by the non-byte 2.30 ms.
2. **The 2.30 ms has never been decomposed.** It is a difference, not a measured phase. No per-phase number
   in the table below accounts for it.

## 2. Ranked pools

Ranked by the size of the pool against the 0.209 ms (device basis) that 300 tok/s needs. Pool size is not a
gain: nothing here is claimed until a paired measurement says so.

**1. The undecomposed non-byte time, ~2300 us.** The only pool of the needed magnitude. It contains the waits,
the publication chains, the queue fetches and the gaps between phases. Two of its intervals are provably
invisible today: producer arrival and the next queue fetch, both of which happen between one task's `done` and
the next task's `taken`, i.e. inside the next gap. **Cheapest check:** stamp those two events and regenerate
the phase table; no new measurement principle, minutes of GPU.

**2. Tail, 243 us, 6.8%.** The last layer's attention → argmax cannot overlap with a following layer because
nothing follows it in the token. Part of it may be avoidable by starting LM_HEAD earlier, since LM_HEAD's
single-task span is the largest in the whole table (130 us, 129 us for its busiest task). **Cheapest check:**
the trace already shows the order; no build needed for the first look.

**3. EXPERT_GATE_UP, in-layer span 33.7 us, the largest MoE phase.** Second largest single-task cost (35 us).
Its stage time is 38.3 ms over 7,696 tasks = 5.0 us/task. **Cheapest check:** compare against the same phase
in the neighbouring layers from the trace that already exists.

**4. QKV_FUSED, in-layer 18.6 us, prologue 56.4 ms over 7,992 tasks = 7.1 us/task.** The prologue is larger
per task than the block it precedes. Note the kernel-resource reality: this build already spills (SGPR spill
630–740 across variants) and `docs/STATUS.md:82` records that spill counts do not predict this kernel's speed.

**5. Protocol bookkeeping.** Measured today: F rejected (+7.5 us on the traced QKV stage sum), K rejected
(+9.1 us on the attention stage sum), both failing their pre-registered screen. The scoped analysis of the
event structure (85 global, 1,504 local events) found only bookkeeping-level removals and **no milliseconds**.
Treat the protocol as exhausted until the non-byte decomposition exists.

**6. Bytes, 1238 us.** The rate is already 4.2 TB/s of a 5.3 TB/s peak, and the one attempt to make more of the
working set cacheable measured **18% slower** (`docs/STATUS.md:81`, the reference to the 4 MB per-XCD L2
cliff). Precision stays bf16. Treat bytes as closed.

## 3. Closed — do not re-propose without new evidence

`--coherent-acts` (not faster), `--prefetch-next` (4% slower), `--topk-published` (slower), one launch per
token (3.733 ms, no speed-up), the gate_up logit load before the 8 KB staging (noise, reverted), o_proj row
split (~1% at most, within spread), kv_a replicated, split workers, idle-worker prefetch. F and K are now in
this list too, with their numbers above.

## 4. What 300 tok/s costs, stated plainly

- device basis: 3.542 → 3.3333 needs **0.209 ms**, 5.9% of the step.
- wall basis: 3.945 → 3.3333 needs **0.612 ms**, 15.5% of the step.

Nothing in the recorded history has produced 0.209 ms from one lever. The largest single-lever gain on record
is the non-temporal weight stream, **4% for one flag** (3.90 ms, `docs/STATUS.md:70`, about 0.16 ms), then
v0.18's q_c publication at **0.055 ms / 1.5%** (`docs/STATUS.md:80`). The single pool that could still hold
0.209 ms is the 2.30 ms that no phase accounts for, and it is invisible to the current logging. That is why
the next step is instrumentation, not a kernel change.

## 5. Do not be worse than the fleet record

The fleet reference is `3.593 / 3.595 / 3.596 ms`, recorded in `docs/STATUS.md:24` with no raw pairing file.
Today's build measures 3.542 ms, but **across sessions absolute milliseconds are not comparable**, so that is
not yet a win. To make the comparison real: one session, five interleaved pairs, the shipped graph against the
recorded configuration. That measurement is cheap and belongs in the next session's first ten minutes.
